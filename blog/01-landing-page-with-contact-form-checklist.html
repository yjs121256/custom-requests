Landing Page With a Contact Form: What It Needs Before You Launch (No-Code Checklist)

A lot of small businesses, freelancers and new products don't need a full website. They need one page that explains the offer clearly and a form that lets the right people get in touch. Done well, a single landing page can carry a waitlist, an enquiry flow or a pre-launch campaign for months.

Done badly, it looks fine on a laptop, breaks on a phone, and sends form submissions to an inbox nobody checks. This checklist covers what a landing page with a contact form needs before you share the link.

Start with one goal

Every landing page should have one main action, the call to action (CTA). Common goals:

  • Collect enquiries for a service
  • Build a waitlist before launch
  • Take booking requests
  • Capture leads for a B2B offer

Write the goal down in one sentence before choosing a template. If you have two goals, you probably need two pages, or one clearly primary goal and a secondary link.

A simple section structure

You rarely need more than eight sections. A structure that works for most offers:

  1. Hero: a clear headline, one supporting line and the CTA button.
  2. Problem: the situation your visitor is in, in their words.
  3. Offer: what you provide and who it's for.
  4. How it works: three short steps.
  5. Details: what's included, pricing or "from" pricing if you share it.
  6. Proof you can honestly show: credentials, a product photo you own, a guarantee policy. Don't invent testimonials.
  7. FAQ: the questions people ask before contacting you.
  8. Form: the contact or signup form, repeated CTA.

Copy that does the work

Visitors skim. The headline should say what you do and for whom, not just sound good. Use short paragraphs, plain words and one idea per section. If you are writing in a second language, have someone check the final text, since small errors in the hero section are noticed most.

The form: fewer fields, better data

Every extra field lowers the chance someone finishes the form. Ask only for what you need to reply:

  • Name
  • Email
  • One question that qualifies the enquiry (for example, "What do you need help with?" or a dropdown of services)

Then test the boring parts that cause the most problems:

  • Does the notification email actually arrive? Check spam.
  • Who receives it, and will they see it?
  • Is there a confirmation message or thank-you page so visitors know it worked?
  • Do you want submissions saved somewhere besides email, such as a spreadsheet? Check whether your builder supports this natively.

Mobile first

Most visitors may arrive from a phone, especially from social media links. Check on a real phone: text size, button size, spacing, and whether the form is easy to fill with a thumb. Many builders show a mobile preview, but a real device catches more.

Meta tags and sharing

When your link is shared in a chat or social post, the platform reads the page's title, description and share image. Set these yourself so the preview looks intentional. The page title and description also appear in search results.

Domain and accounts

  • Build the page in your own builder account (Carrd, Framer or similar) so you keep control. Invite a helper as a collaborator rather than sharing your password.
  • Check whether forms and custom domains need a paid plan on your builder.
  • Connecting a custom domain usually means adding DNS records at your domain registrar. Keep the instructions somewhere you can find them later.

Pre-launch checklist

  • [ ] One goal and one primary CTA
  • [ ] Headline says what and for whom
  • [ ] Sections in a logical order, no filler
  • [ ] Form tested: notification received, confirmation shown
  • [ ] Checked on a real phone
  • [ ] Page title, description and share image set
  • [ ] Domain connected and HTTPS working
  • [ ] You can edit the page yourself later
