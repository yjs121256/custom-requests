How to Find a Korean Skincare OEM/ODM Manufacturer: A Step-by-Step Sourcing Guide

"Made in Korea" carries weight in skincare, and many brands outside Korea want to launch a product produced by a Korean manufacturer. The obstacle is usually practical rather than technical: many manufacturer websites, catalogues and replies are in Korean, and it's hard to tell from abroad which companies fit a small or new brand.

This guide explains how the sourcing process works and how to approach it in an organised way.

OEM, ODM or private label?

These terms are used loosely, so be clear about what you mean when you contact a maker:

  • OEM: the manufacturer produces your formula to your specification.
  • ODM: the manufacturer develops or supplies the formula, and you brand it. Often faster for a first product.
  • Private label: usually an existing product from the maker's range with your branding and packaging.

If you're not sure yet, say so. Many makers will explain which options they offer.

Prepare before you contact anyone

Manufacturers prioritise enquiries that are clear and look serious. Before reaching out, write down:

  1. Product type and format: for example, a vitamin C serum in 30 ml, a sunscreen or a sheet mask.
  2. Target markets: where you'll sell affects regulations, labelling and sometimes the formula.
  3. Order size: the minimum order quantity (MOQ) you can handle and, if known, a target cost per unit.
  4. Must-haves: vegan, specific certifications, packaging type, launch date.
  5. What you can share: your brand name and how much detail about your plans you're comfortable disclosing.

Build a shortlist from public sources

Start wide, then narrow. For each candidate, record:

  • Company name and main product types
  • Certifications they state publicly, such as CGMP or ISO 22716
  • Export markets they mention
  • Official contact page or enquiry form
  • The source link for every fact

Keep this in a table. Treat anything a company says about itself as "stated", not "verified", until you've seen documentation.

Write a clear quote request

A good first enquiry is short and specific: who you are, what product, target market, intended order size, and the questions you need answered. Writing in Korean helps, because it's easier for the team to read and forward internally. Send it once to the official contact, not to multiple addresses at the same company.

Key questions to ask:

  • What is the MOQ for this product type?
  • What is the approximate unit price range at that quantity?
  • What does sampling cost, and how long does it take?
  • What is the production lead time?
  • Do you support OEM, ODM or both?

Compare replies side by side

Replies arrive in different formats and levels of detail. Put them into one comparison table: MOQ, unit price range, sample cost, lead time and notes. Mark anything unclear as an open question for a follow-up.

Set realistic expectations

  • Not everyone replies. Some makers respond slowly or only to orders above a certain size.
  • Prices are ranges. Final pricing depends on formula, packaging and quantity.
  • Desk research isn't an audit. If quality and compliance matter for your market, plan for proper checks before committing, and confirm regulatory requirements with a qualified expert.

Red flags

  • No verifiable company information or official contact
  • Claims of certifications with no way to confirm them
  • Pressure to pay large amounts before samples
  • Answers that change between messages
