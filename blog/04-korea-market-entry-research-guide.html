Korea Market Entry Research: What to Check Before You Sell in South Korea

South Korea is attractive for many product categories: online shopping is a normal part of daily life, consumers follow trends closely, and Korean brands are strong in categories like skincare and food. It's also a market where foreign brands often struggle at first, because most of the useful information (competitor pages, marketplace listings, customer reviews, news) is in Korean.

Before you spend on inventory, ads or a local partner, a few weeks of focused desk research can answer the questions that decide whether and how to enter. Here's what to look at.

Start with the decision

Research without a decision behind it becomes a pile of facts. Write down the question you need answered:

  • Should we enter Korea at all this year?
  • Which sales channel should we test first?
  • What price can we realistically charge?
  • How should we position against local brands?

Every part of the research should serve that question.

1. Who already sells something similar

Search the Korean names for your product category, not just the English ones. For each competitor, note:

  • Brand and product
  • Price point and pack size
  • Where they sell
  • How they describe themselves: the claims, words and benefits they lead with

Include both Korean brands and foreign brands already present. If foreign competitors are selling successfully, look at how they adapted.

2. Sales channels

Korean online retail has its own landscape. Channels to evaluate, depending on your product, include Naver Smart Store, Coupang, Kakao, Instagram and YouTube, plus category-specific shops. For each, think about how products are found, what competitors do there, and what it would take for you to sell there. Note where your competitors are concentrated and where they're absent.

3. Prices

Collect real listed prices for comparable products, including pack size, so you can compare per unit. Note promotions and bundles, which are common. This tells you whether your planned price sits at the premium end, the middle or below local options.

4. What customers say

Korean marketplace reviews are a rich source of information. Read through reviews of competing products and group them into themes: what customers praise, what they complain about, which features or claims they mention. Recurring complaints about competitors can point to an opening for you.

5. Rules to confirm

Depending on the category, there may be certification, registration or labelling requirements (for example, KC certification for some products or MFDS rules for cosmetics and food). Desk research can identify which rules may apply and link to official sources, but always confirm them with a licensed expert or agency before importing.

6. Local wording

How competitors phrase their benefits in Korean gives you ready-made insight into what matters to buyers. A few short Korean copy lines to test, based on that language, are a practical output of good research.

Keep every claim sourced

The most useful research reports link each key claim to its source, so you can check anything that looks surprising and update the report later. Anything that can't be confirmed should be marked as unconfirmed rather than stated as fact.
